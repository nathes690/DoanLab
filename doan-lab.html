The Doan Lab

A metagenomic epidemiology laboratory at the UCSF Proctor Foundation. Samples reach us from clinics, hospitals, and field research centers around the world — from Niger and Burkina Faso to India, Nepal, Thailand, Vietnam, Vanuatu, Chile, and across the United States. We study how the human microbiome responds to change, and we use sequencing to name the cause of an eye infection when conventional testing cannot.

Principal Investigator

Thuy Doan, MD, PhD

Associate Professor

Dr. Doan leads the lab's work on ocular infection and inflammation, microbiomes, and antimicrobial resistance. Her research asks why mass antibiotic distribution improves child survival in sub-Saharan Africa, and what that treatment costs in resistance, so that the answer can inform public health policy rather than sit in a journal.

Alongside that population work, she applies the same sequencing tools one patient at a time, identifying the organisms behind uveitis, conjunctivitis, corneal ulcers, and scleritis.

Our Work

What the lab does

Three lines of work, one method. Samples are collected inside randomized trials and clinics rather than generated in the laboratory, then sequenced without assuming in advance what they contain.

 

Mechanism

Why mass treatment saves children

Distributing azithromycin to preschool children in sub-Saharan Africa lowers childhood mortality. We look at the gut, ocular, and upper respiratory microbiomes to work out how.

 

Surveillance

Tracking antimicrobial resistance

Any benefit from mass treatment has to be weighed against resistance. We track it in the same communities over time, so policy rests on measurement rather than assumption.

 

Diagnosis

Naming the cause of an eye infection

Unbiased sequencing identifies pathogens in uveitis, conjunctivitis, corneal ulcers, and scleritis, including cases where culture and targeted testing return nothing.

Research

Current projects

Four active studies, spanning corneal pathogens, uveitis tissue, mass drug distribution, and a global look at conjunctivitis epidemics.

  • capriCORN

    Comprehensive Analysis of Pathogens, Resistomes, and Inflammatory-markers in the CORNea.

  • LOTUS

    Leveraging Ocular Tissues in Uveitis Study — working with partners at home and abroad on how ocular inflammatory disease arises, and on better classification, diagnosis, and biomarkers.

  • MORDOR / AVENIR

    Large randomized trials testing whether mass drug distribution improves childhood survival in Niger and Burkina Faso.

  • SCORPIO

    An international, multi-site study of conjunctivitis epidemics: which pathogens drive them, how their genomes shift across time and place, and how the immune system answers.
